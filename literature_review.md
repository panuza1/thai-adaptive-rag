# Literature review

Search date: 2026-10-29. Sources: original papers on arXiv/ACL, authors' repositories, publisher DOI records. This is a focused initial review, not a systematic review. Bibliographic identities and linked abstracts were checked; where full-text experimental detail was not inspected, it is explicitly pending. Limitations below include our assessment, not necessarily authors' stated limitations. No priority or novelty claim is established.

## Dataset and model sources (not peer-reviewed paper claims)

- iApp Technology, **iapp-wiki-qa-dataset**: https://github.com/iapp-technology/iapp-wiki-qa-dataset . Repository advertises 1,961 documents and 9,170 questions. Our input is only its SQuAD-format training file; these totals must not be substituted for observed input counts. Repository license MIT; Wikipedia text attribution obligations should be reviewed before redistribution. Do not retain contributor/payment metadata in processed data.
- Multilingual E5 small: https://huggingface.co/intfloat/multilingual-e5-small . Use `query: ` and `passage: ` prefixes, normalized embeddings, and record commit revision. Audit training overlap with MIRACL before using it as independent evidence.
- Cross encoder: https://huggingface.co/cross-encoder/mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1 . Multilingual architecture is not proof of Thai reranking quality; evaluate empirically.

## Gap, questions, hypotheses and contribution

**Candidate gap:** the measured value of inexpensive, interpretable routing among dense, lexical-dense fusion and cross-encoder reranking for Thai QA under a fixed generator and evidence budget. This is an empirical scope, not proof that no previous Thai work exists. Broaden Thai-language database searches and complete the nearest-paper full-text comparison before claiming novelty.

RQ1: Can P1 reduce mean retrieval latency relative to B3 while retaining answer correctness within a prespecified tolerance? RQ2: What retrieval and evidence-grounding tradeoffs result? RQ3: Which feature and component removals change routing quality? RQ4: Is there enough predictable routing headroom to justify a learned router?

H1: P1 has lower mean retrieval latency than B3. H2: P1 answer correctness is non-inferior to B3 within 0.03 absolute, subject to advisor approval before test access. H3: confidence and lexical features improve the development quality-cost frontier relative to their ablations. These hypotheses may fail. A nonsignificant difference does not establish non-inferiority.

Proposed contributions: reproducible Thai protocol; transparent routing policy with measured overhead; paired quality-cost and error analysis, including negative outcomes. Limitations: Wikipedia scope, partial evidence annotation, model pretraining contamination, heuristic complexity/exact-token proxies, hardware-specific timing, human annotation requirements, and incomplete literature coverage.

| **AB-RAG: Adaptive Budgeted Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Reliable Question Answering**; Ansh Kamthan; 2026 preprint; https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29090 | Training-free confidence-guided stopping under retrieval budget | Two datasets; exact names pending full-text inspection | EM, confidence stratification, retrieval budget/cost | Reports backbone-dependent benefits and negative results | Another closely related efficiency/reliability study; empirical claims need independent replication |

Revised positioning: not the first Thai hybrid system, not the first Thai query-dependent retriever, and not the first cheap feature router. The defensible deliverable is a reproducible controlled evaluation of a specific transparent discrete policy, with explicit routing overhead and reliability uncertainty. Its publication value depends on what the experiments reveal and how carefully they rule out trivial fixed-policy alternatives.

**NitiBench: A Comprehensive Study of LLM Framework Capabilities for Thai Legal Question Answering** — Pawitsapak Akarajaradwong, Pirat Pothavorn, Chompakorn Chaksangchaichot, Panuthep Tasawong, Thitiwat Nopparatbundit, Keerakiat Pratai, Sarana Nutanong (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10868 . Method: compare RAG and long-context approaches, legal section chunking and cross-reference expansion. Datasets: NitiBench-CCL and NitiBench-Tax. Metrics: tailored multilabel retrieval measures and judge-based coverage/contradiction evaluation. Findings: legal structure-aware chunking helps, while complex queries remain difficult. Limitations/relevance: legal domain and judge dependence require care; useful additional Thai dataset and evidence-grounding protocol. Author list verified against the current original abstract, rather than copied from another paper's bibliography.

Royal Gazette availability follow-up: the original paper identifies https://huggingface.co/datasets/VISAI-AI/thai-gazette-evidence-retrieval and states CC BY 4.0. Its questions include synthetic construction; it is not interchangeable with human-authored iApp QA. Dataset contents and release availability still need direct inspection before ingestion. Legal RAG full-text follow-up: Promwang and Boonma's hybrid weights depend on retrieval confidence and cutoff k; this is especially relevant prior work for our confidence feature. Discrete selection must demonstrate savings beyond merely adjusting fusion weights.
